# Naming principles

## Source
Summary of the brand-name psychology paper the user works with
(https://assets.ctfassets.net/sm4pjcjvw1eb/bW9Qh71QLBOmQ4ItyBEWf/93a3d24a251826cad1012f7fee8242c4/Brand_Names.pdf)
plus the user's own naming process. Re-read the PDF when in doubt.

## Name categories (strongest → weakest as a brand, roughly)
- **Neologistic / invented** (Google, Kodak, Zalando): no meaning yet,
  fully protectable, scales to an umbrella brand. Hardest to make sound good.
- **Deviant / unrelated real word** (Apple, Dove, Notion): memorable and
  emotional. Almost all short English nouns are taken as .de/.app/.com.
- **Suggestive / evocative** (Solaris – sun, Stripe): hints at a quality.
- **Compound / blend** (Flixbus, Salesforce).
- **Descriptive** (Groupware AG, "aireadygroupware"): weak, hard to protect,
  boxes the company in. Avoid for an umbrella brand.

## Sound and form
- Short: 2–3 syllables, 4–7 letters. Easy to spell after hearing it once
  (telephone test: "Wie schreibt man das?" must not be needed).
- Stress on the first syllable.
- Plosive start (b, d, g, k, p, t) feels strong and memorable.
- Consonants moving front → back in the mouth (p/b → t/d → k/g) feel natural.
- Front vowels (i, e) = small, light, fast; back vowels (o, u, a) = big,
  solid, warm.
- Avoid letter combinations whose pronunciation is ambiguous in the target
  market (sv-/sw-, v as f or w, c as k or ts) — or decide it once.

## The user's process (for reference)
1. Read the paper.
2. Decide which name categories to exclude.
3. Brainstorm doc: concepts, words, emotions, prefixes/suffixes (Wikipedia
   affix lists).
4. Combine wildly, rank by the paper's rules → 50 ideas.
5. Feed the AI this guide + the concept doc → 50 more.
6. Shortlist the ones you could warm up to.
7. Top 10 into a spreadsheet (domain, pronunciation, meaning, conflicts, …).
8. Fill it; drop names without fitting domain or with bad pronunciation;
   refill until 3–7 good options remain. Conflict search:
   1. domain check (+ look at content of registered domains)
   2. web search in several spellings
   3. DPMA trademark search
   4. AI: "I once heard a brand that sounded like XYZ — help me find it"
   5. AI: "How could XYZ be read negatively? In other languages? How would
      someone make fun of it?"
9. Sleep on it. Vote yourself (e.g. 3 votes), then let people from the
   target group vote.
10. Buy the domain.
